Features, and what each one is for

Twelve capabilities that don't appear in the systems above, and the reason each exists.

Recency as the headline number

Share of open accounts that paid anything in the last 30 days, measured against a target.

Aging buckets are a lender's metric. BHPH portfolios are managed on recency, and the industry target sits in the low-to-mid 90s.

Collection potential

Dollars collected in a period against dollars contractually due. Above 100% means catch-up money is arriving on top of scheduled payments.

It is the measure of the payment window itself, not of individual accounts.

Per diem during the call

Daily interest on the account, and how many days of interest a given payment covers.

Other systems show interest and principal in the payment history — after the fact. This puts it in front of the collector while the customer is still on the line.

Payment-misses-principal warning

Flags any payment that goes entirely to interest and fees, and states the smallest amount that would reduce the balance.

On a simple-interest note a chronically late customer can pay for a year without the balance moving. Neither party usually notices.

Extension priced before it's granted

Shows the accrued interest an extension adds and the new payoff, with an approver and a per-contract limit.

An extension buys time, not money. The number makes that concrete for both sides.

Collector scorecard

Kept-promise rate, right-party contact rate, recency within each collector's own accounts, cash collected per account.

These are the measures BHPH collector compensation is typically written against.

Enforced contact limits

Call frequency cap, contact-hours lockout, texting consent with an expiry, opt-out and cease-contact. The buttons stop working and state the reason.

Every limit is a setting the dealership's attorney provides. The software enforces; it does not interpret.

Notice merge

Merges account data into attorney-supplied notice text, computes the cure date, records certified mail tracking, and prints.

Merging a name and a date into approved text is clerical. Drafting the text is not, and the software does not attempt it.

Examination trail

Every contact, promise, payment, notice and consent event exported as one chronological file.

Texas requires records held until the later of four years from the transaction or two years from the last entry.

Recovery pipeline

Six stages from decision to resale, with tow cost, recon, asking price and the new down payment recorded against the original note.

A lot that owns its wrecker and re-retails its own units is not writing off a repossession — it is moving inventory. The shortfall is measured against re-retail, not auction.

Bilingual scripts

Eleven texts — including a pre-due reminder that goes out before an account is ever late — and four call scripts written in both languages, auto-selected by how late the account is, and refused outright when the account can't be texted.

Most of this lot's customers are Spanish-speaking. The Spanish is written, not machine-translated from the English.

Window receipt

Two bilingual copies on one page, printed at the counter or reprinted later from any past payment.

Cash crosses the counter here. A customer with nothing in her hand cannot prove she paid, and the lot has no signed record that she did.

What this tool does not do

Stated plainly, because a comparison that only lists strengths isn't a comparison.

  • It is not a dealer management system. No inventory, deal desking, titles, forms, or general ledger. It reads a CSV from whatever DMS is in place and writes one back.
  • The DMS never synchronises live. Aging comes in by file — since v6.5 the morning import folds into the board instead of replacing it — the board goes out by file, and payments are entered twice, here and in the DMS.
  • Card data is deliberately out of reach. Cards are typed on Authorize.net's own hosted page, never in this app. That keeps the dealership out of PCI scope and it is not going to change.
  • An online payment is recorded, not auto-applied. The webhook says money arrived; a person taps once to apply it. That is on purpose — a replayed webhook or a partial payment gets a human look before it moves a balance.
  • The demo build is per-device, and it neither texts nor takes payments. The standalone version keeps everything in one browser with no login and no network — useful for showing people, not for running a lot with two collectors. The server build on Supabase is where the shared board, logins, Twilio texting, and Authorize.net card payments live.
  • It does not write legal language and does not generate post-repossession notices.
  • It does not report to credit bureaus. The data is all there; the Metro 2 file is not built, because wrong information on a real person's credit file is not a defect you ship and patch later. It needs the current spec and bureau certification.
  • There is no support line. The vendors above answer the phone during business hours. This has no equivalent, and for a lot that depends on its DMS that difference is worth real money.

Method and sources

  1. Feature marks reflect vendor documentation, published support material and software directories as of August 2026. A dash indicates the capability is not documented publicly.   4. Recovery economics are modelled on re-retail rather than auction disposition, which is appropriate for a dealership that recovers and reconditions its own units and inappropriate for one that does not.
